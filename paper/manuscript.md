# HWO Candidate-Target Catalog Cross-Match: Methods and Results (v0.3.0)

**Author**: Biswajit Jana

## Abstract

We cross-match the NASA Exoplanet Archive's 164-target Direct Imaging ExEP
Mission Star List (Mamajek & Stapelfeldt 2024) against the Hipparcos-Gaia
Catalog of Accelerations (HGCA; Brandt 2021) to report, per star and per
category, whether real observational coverage exists for Habitable Worlds
Observatory (HWO) target vetting. Four categories count as real coverage:
stellar parameters, chromospheric activity, multiplicity (Washington
Double Star catalog cross-match), and astrometric acceleration (HGCA
entry). Two further archive fields (known-planet flag, circumstellar-disk
flag populated) are tracked separately and deliberately excluded from the
completeness score, since they are populated for essentially every target
in this list regardless of whether any real companion or exozodi
observation was performed. Catalog-field availability is 164/164 for stellar
parameters (Wilson 95% CI 97.7%-100%), 163/164 for activity (96.6%-99.9%),
160/164 for astrometric acceleration (93.9%-99.0%), and 109/164 for
multiplicity (58.9%-73.2%); mean `evidence_completeness` across the four
coverage categories is 90.9%. This average is a catalog-field availability
summary, not HWO target-list completeness, mission readiness, or
direct-imaging detectability.
Cross-matching the two catalogs surfaces 31 targets (28 after
Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across all 164
comparisons) with a statistically significant astrometric acceleration
(p < 0.01, 2 degrees of freedom) but no archive-listed planet -- candidates
for follow-up characterization, not a confirmed catalog conflict or
detection, since an archive-listed-planet flag of "N" does not establish
that a star is single. Evidence completeness and scientific value (here,
proxied by distance) are reported as two permanently separate ranked
views, never merged into one score, because merging them would hide
exactly the "high value, low evidence" targets this project exists to
surface.

## 1. Introduction

The Habitable Worlds Observatory's direct-imaging exo-Earth survey depends
on pre-launch stellar characterization -- multiplicity, activity, and
companion information all affect both target selection and exposure-time
planning. Mamajek & Stapelfeldt (2024) compiled 164 promising targets;
this project asks a narrower, complementary question: for exactly that
list, which evidence categories are actually populated, and where do two
independent catalogs (the archive's own compilation and the HGCA's
astrometric acceleration measurements) already disagree?

## 3. Validation

See `docs/VALIDATION.md`. An initial release-time bug (a wide-binary
shared-Hipparcos-number merge fan-out inside the pipeline's internal HGCA
join) was caught and fixed by running the full pipeline against live data
before that publication. A subsequent external review (Codex, 2026-08-29)
found a second, more consequential instance of the same underlying class
of bug in `combined_view`'s separate merge, which had inflated
`results/ranking.csv` to 174 rows (164 unique stars, 10 duplicated rows
from 5 wide-binary pairs); it also found that `evidence_completeness`
included two archive fields that are populated for essentially every
target regardless of real coverage. Both are fixed in this release (a
stable `target_id` now backs every merge; the two field-populated
categories are excluded from the completeness score) -- see
`docs/CLAIMS.md` "Superseded / corrected claims" and `CHANGELOG.md` for
the full account.

## 4. Results

All numbers below are read directly from `results/evidence_matrix.csv` and
`results/hgca_accelerators_without_listed_planets.csv`, generated by
`hwoevidence build` (`docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`); none are typed by hand.

**Per-category evidence** (164 targets; the first four rows are real
observational coverage and feed into `evidence_completeness` -- mean
90.9%; the last two are archive-bookkeeping fields, tracked but excluded):

| Category | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Stellar parameters | 100.0% |
| Activity (log R'HK) | 99.4% |
| Astrometric acceleration (HGCA entry) | 97.6% |
| Multiplicity (WDS cross-match) | 66.5% |
| Archive planet-flag populated (not coverage) | 100.0% |
| Archive disk-flag populated (not coverage) | 100.0% |

**HGCA accelerators without an archive-listed planet**: 31/164 targets
(18.9%) at raw p < 0.01, or 28/164 (17.1%) after Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
correction across all 164 comparisons
(`results/hgca_accelerators_without_listed_planets.csv`). This is not a
confirmed catalog conflict -- `sy_planets_flag == "N"` means no
archive-listed planet, not a confirmed single star. Of the 160 targets with an
HGCA entry, 25.0% (raw) / 21.9% (FDR-corrected) show a significant
acceleration overall (not all of which lack an archive-listed planet --
see the distinction in `docs/METHODS.md`).

## 5. What this result does and does not show

This demonstrates that (a) the two catalogs can be reliably cross-matched
at scale, (b) evidence gaps are unevenly distributed across categories
(multiplicity is the clear outlier), and (c) a meaningful fraction of
targets have companion-related evidence in tension between sources. It
does **not** establish the nature, mass, or period of any specific
candidate companion, does not assess the ~13,000-star HPIC catalog beyond
this 164-target subset, and does not incorporate UV/X-ray high-energy-
radiation evidence or dedicated exozodi-survey (HOSTS) results, both
identified as real, citable, not-yet-integrated extensions
(`docs/DATA_SOURCES.md`, `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`).

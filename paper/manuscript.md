# HWO Target Evidence Cross-Match: Methods and Results (v0.1.0)

**Author**: Biswajit Jana

## Abstract

We cross-match the NASA Exoplanet Archive's 164-target Direct Imaging ExEP
Mission Star List (Mamajek & Stapelfeldt 2024) against the Hipparcos-Gaia
Catalog of Accelerations (HGCA; Brandt 2021) to report, per star and per
evidence category, whether the pre-launch characterization measurement
needed for Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) target vetting exists at all.
Six categories are scored: stellar parameters, chromospheric activity,
multiplicity (Washington Double Star catalog cross-match), known
companions, exozodiacal-dust assessment, and astrometric acceleration.
Stellar-parameter, known-companion, and exozodi-flag evidence are
essentially complete (100%, 100%, 100%); activity evidence is 99.4%
complete; HGCA entries exist for 97.6% of targets; multiplicity evidence
covers only 66.5%. Cross-matching the two catalogs' independent
companion-related evidence surfaces 31 targets with a statistically
significant astrometric acceleration (p < 0.01, 2 degrees of freedom) but
no companion flagged in the archive's own list -- candidates for follow-up
characterization, not confirmed detections. Evidence completeness and
scientific value (here, proxied by distance) are reported as two
permanently separate ranked views, never merged into one score, because
merging them would hide exactly the "high value, low evidence" targets
this project exists to surface.

## 1. Introduction

The Habitable Worlds Observatory's direct-imaging exo-Earth survey depends
on pre-launch stellar characterization -- multiplicity, activity, and
companion information all affect both target selection and exposure-time
planning. Mamajek & Stapelfeldt (2024) compiled 164 promising targets;
this project asks a narrower, complementary question: for exactly that
list, which evidence categories are actually populated, and where do two
independent catalogs (the archive's own compilation and the HGCA's
astrometric acceleration measurements) already disagree?

## 4. Results

All numbers below are read directly from `results/evidence_matrix.csv` and
`results/conflicts.csv`, generated by `hwoevidence build`
(`docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`); none are typed by hand.

**Per-category evidence completeness** (164 targets):

| Category | Completeness |
|---|---|
| Stellar parameters | 100.0% |
| Known companions (flag assessed) | 100.0% |
| Exozodiacal dust (flag assessed) | 100.0% |
| Activity (log R'HK) | 99.4% |
| Astrometric acceleration (HGCA entry) | 97.6% |
| Multiplicity (WDS cross-match) | 66.5% |

**Conflicts**: 31/164 targets (18.9%) have a statistically significant
HGCA acceleration (p < 0.01) with no companion flagged by the archive
(`results/conflicts.csv`). Of the targets with an HGCA entry, 24.4% show a
significant acceleration overall (not all of which lack a known
companion -- see the distinction in `docs/METHODS.md`).

## 5. What this result does and does not show

This demonstrates that (a) the two catalogs can be reliably cross-matched
at scale, (b) evidence gaps are unevenly distributed across categories
(multiplicity is the clear outlier), and (c) a meaningful fraction of
targets have companion-related evidence in tension between sources. It
does **not** establish the nature, mass, or period of any specific
candidate companion, does not assess the ~13,000-star HPIC catalog beyond
this 164-target subset, and does not incorporate UV/X-ray high-energy-
radiation evidence or dedicated exozodi-survey (HOSTS) results, both
identified as real, citable, not-yet-integrated extensions
(`docs/DATA_SOURCES.md`, `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`).
